# The Open Tools Pledge

A public, personal commitment. Take it by signing below — the act of promising *is* the mechanism that lets others rely on your tools.

## The commitment

> **The tools I make stay open. I charge only for performance.**

Breaking it down:

1. **My tools are open.** Anything I build that is a *tool* — a reusable enabler, a pattern, a layer, a schema, a library — is released with an open license and available to everyone, now and in the future.
2. **Performance is what I sell.** I earn from the *doing* — the products, services, and work that use the tools — never from locking the tools away.
3. **I improve the tools I depend on.** Because my performance rides on them, contributing to them is my own business, not a favour.
4. **I keep finding the next layer.** Wherever a repeated pattern hides inside a performance, I separate it and open it.

## Why make a public pledge

- Promises convert an idea into a *cooperative game*: others can rely on your tools being open, so they can build entire businesses on them without fear.
- The pledge is the "commitment device" that makes the good outcome of the game stable. One closed tool can break trust for everyone; pledges rebuild it.
- It is the practical first move of the doctrine: **be first to provide open tools, and promise you will not close them.**

## How it binds

This is a moral and social pledge, not a legal one. Its power is that it is **public and dated**: breaking it is breaking a stated promise, visible to everyone who relied on it. If you prefer a legal commitment for a specific project, pair the pledge with a permissive open-source license on the tool and a paid-services contract on the performance.

## Spread it

The answer only does its work in people's heads. When you take the pledge, share it. Copy `PLEDGE.md` into your own repos as `CODE_OF_CONDUCT` or `COMMITMENT.md`, or just tell people the one line:

> **"The tools I make stay open. I charge only for performance."**
